# Evaluation Methodology

This document describes how the repository can support PhD-level empirical research on human-AI collaboration.

## Study conditions

A controlled study can compare three conditions:

| Condition | Description |
|---|---|
| Human-only | Participant completes the task without AI assistance. |
| AI-only | AI output is evaluated without human intervention. |
| Human-AI | Participant reviews, accepts, rejects, or revises AI suggestions. |

## Suggested measurements

| Construct | Possible measurement |
|---|---|
| Decision quality | Expert rubric, correctness, defect detection rate, or task score. |
| Efficiency | Completion time, review time, number of attempts, or interaction count. |
| Trust | Post-task trust survey, acceptance pattern, confidence alignment. |
| Cognitive load | Self-report scale, task difficulty rating, or qualitative feedback. |
| Explainability | Participant rating of usefulness, clarity, and evidence quality. |
| Collaboration risk | Frequency of automation bias, verification gaps, or low explainability. |

## Research procedure

1. Define task scenarios and expected outcomes.
2. Run a pilot study to calibrate difficulty.
3. Collect baseline human-only performance.
4. Generate or simulate AI recommendations.
5. Run human-AI assisted trials.
6. Record decisions, outcomes, confidence, and qualitative notes.
7. Compute collaboration metrics.
8. Interpret results using both quantitative and qualitative evidence.

## Validity threats

- Small participant sample.
- Unrealistic task scenarios.
- Learning effects across conditions.
- Overly simplified quality scores.
- Lack of expert ground truth.
- Incomplete measurement of cognitive load.

## Reporting recommendation

Research reports should clearly separate AI output quality from human-AI collaboration quality. A strong AI suggestion does not automatically mean good collaboration, and a rejected suggestion may still be useful if it improves human reasoning.
